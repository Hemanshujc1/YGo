# YGo

Hyperlocal commerce platform connecting customers with local shops within roughly 2-3 km. In Phase 1 each shop delivers with its own staff.

This document carries the domain: what the product does and what the words mean. **Technical and stack decisions live in `docs/adr/`** — one record per stack ticket, indexed in `docs/adr/README.md` — and are not repeated here beyond the few places a platform limit changes a product rule.

`docs/product/revenue-system-spec.md` is authoritative for pricing and settlement. `docs/product/prd-phase1.md` is the Phase 1 requirements document; it summarises and points here, and this document governs where the two disagree. `docs/product/prd-phase1-reference.md` is an earlier PRD for the same product under a retired brand: a reference input only, overridden by this document and by any closed decision.

## Language

**Shop**: A local business that sells to customers. Never "store".
**Owner**: The person who owns a shop and has full access to it.
**Staff**: A person working for one shop. One role, not several. Staff handle orders, manage stock, run counter billing, add products from the master catalog, and deliver when their `can deliver` flag is set.
**`can deliver`**: A per-staff flag, not a role. It reveals the delivery screens in the Shopkeeper app and puts the person in the delivery-assignment list.
**Customer**: A person ordering from shops. Never "user".
**Admin**: The platform operator.
**Area Manager**: The operator of exactly one Area. Onboards and supports the shops in it, and is paid a share of the platform commission earned there. The mockups use a retired term for this role; use Area Manager everywhere in current requirements and implementation.
**Area**: An admin-named container of one or more pincodes, used for administration. Never "zone".
**Service radius**: A shop's own delivery distance. A shop is shown to a customer who is inside it, and the customer's Area does not have to be the shop's Area. It is the second of two tests: the customer's location must first be in an active Area (see Service area).
**Area pause**: An admin switch that stops new orders in an Area while live orders finish. Separate from a shop's open/closed toggle.
**Location**: Only a geographic location, such as an address or map coordinate.
**Order**: A customer's purchase from exactly one shop. Delivery fee, stock, coupon and handover OTP are per order.
**Cart**: A customer's items for one shop. A customer can hold one cart per shop and checks out one shop at a time.
**Bargain**: A customer's offer of a price per unit for a chosen quantity on a bargain-enabled product, matched instantly against the shop's hidden floor price.
**Floor price**: The Owner's hidden minimum price per unit for a bargain-enabled product. Never shown to customers.
**Slot**: An owner-defined delivery time window with a maximum order capacity, used by Scheduled delivery.
**Master product**: An admin-owned catalog entry: name, brand, category, image, optional barcode, and its packs. Shops list it; they do not create it.
**Pack**: A canonical sellable size of a master product ("1 kg", "12 bananas"), defined on the master product. A shop chooses which packs it stocks and prices each one. Price, stock, bargain toggle and floor price all sit on the shop's pack row.
**Quantity-price option**: A shop's own priced quantity on its listing ("2 pav for Rs 6"), carrying a quantity in the master's base unit. Additional to packs, never a replacement for them, and it holds no stock of its own.
**Reservation**: Stock held for an accepted order. Available stock is on-hand minus reserved. A reservation becomes a decrement on delivery or pickup and is released on any other terminal state.
**Commission**: The platform's share of a delivered order's eligible product value. Delivered orders only; pickup orders and counter bills carry none.
**Booking fee**: A fee on a completed self-pickup order, charged to the customer and optionally to the shop. Never called a delivery fee.
**Contribution floor**: The minimum platform contribution an order must produce to be placeable. Configured per fulfilment route.
**Statement**: A shop's weekly record of what it owes the platform. Money flows from shop to platform, never the reverse except by authorised manual adjustment.
**Bill reference**: The identifier a counter bill is given on the phone at the moment of sale. Permanent, unique, and shown on every copy of the bill.
**Bill number**: A counter bill's place in the shop's unbroken sequence, given by the server when the bill reaches it. A bill made offline has a reference at once and a number later.
**Rate card**: The pricing values in force for one shop: commission, delivery fee and its shares, floors, minimum cart and limits. A shop is shown its rate card before it goes Live.
**Join code**: A one-time code the Owner shows a new Staff member, who enters it to link their account to the shop.
**Unverified phone number**: A number its holder typed at sign-up and nobody has checked. Every Customer's and Staff member's number in stage one, and an Owner's before the visit.
**Visit-confirmed phone number**: An Owner's number that Admin has confirmed, in person at the shop visit, belongs to the person in the shop. A person's word and a record, never a code sent to the phone.
**SMS-verified phone number**: A number confirmed by a code sent to it. No account has one until SMS sign-in is switched on in stage two.
**Shopkeeper app**: The mobile surface for Owner and Staff. "Shopkeeper" is only a surface name.

## Retired and banned terms

**Store**, **Zone**: retired. Use Shop and Area.
**Billing staff**, **Delivery staff**: retired as roles. There is one Staff role plus the `can deliver` flag.
**Partner**: banned outright, bare or qualified. The mockups used it for both the area manager and the delivery rider. Use **Area Manager**. A future platform-managed rider network will get its own name when it is in scope.

Customer Mobile, Shopkeeper Mobile and Admin Web. Shopkeeper Web and a separate Delivery Staff app are deferred; the delivery screens are a tab inside the Shopkeeper app.

## Platforms, devices, language and themes

**Both Android and iOS are in scope from day one, from one codebase.** Nothing in the design, the component layer or the native surface may assume Android only. The two platforms differ only in their release gates: the **Google Play release gates the soft launch** because of the fourteen-day closed test, while the **Apple submission does not** and goes in as soon as the Apple account clears. The Apple individual enrolment is the one unverified item, costs 99 USD a year, has been reported to fail at the payment step from India, and should be started early so it is a known cost or a known problem rather than a surprise.

**The acceptance alarm is constrained on both platforms, not only on iOS.** iOS limits background audio. Android has since January 2025 granted the full-screen intent by default only to alarm-clock and calling apps, and an audio foreground service needs a Play Console declaration, so neither platform will reliably ring until someone acts. The floor, present on both, is a high-importance notification channel with ALARM usage and a custom sound — a time-sensitive push on iOS — plus the in-app looping alarm whenever the app is open, with the founder phoning the shop as the stage-one backstop. On Android a full-screen alarm is an **enhancement** whose permission is requested during the in-person onboarding and may be refused, never a requirement for correctness. **Pilot shops are therefore still advised to run the Shopkeeper app on an Android phone**, but the advantage is now the possibility of that alarm rather than a guarantee of it; iOS is supported at the counter, not recommended. The Customer app is unaffected.

**Every surface is English only in Phase 1**, with no Devanagari in the interface and **every string externalised from day one** so that Hindi is later a translation pass and never a hunt for hardcoded text. The search layer is separate and already matches Hindi typed in English letters through the synonym table.

**Android 10 is the floor**, covering roughly 95% of Indian Android traffic by web-traffic sampling, which skews new; the real figure is lower and wants a sanity check against the phones seen on the shop visits. **The iOS minimum is 16.4**, set by the toolchain rather than chosen. Mobile is **phone and portrait only** on entry-level hardware, 2 GB RAM and slow storage assumed, with no tablet layout and no landscape. **Admin Web is desktop-first** on an evergreen browser, usable down to tablet width, with no phone layout.

A shop needs **no dedicated device, no device binding and no install limit**: Owner and Staff each sign in on their own phone, each with its own offline cache and queue. The one documented expectation, not an enforced rule, is that the phone kept at the counter permits notifications and sound, because that is the device the acceptance alarm must reach.

**Light and dark both ship**, following the system setting with a per-account override and no pinning to light. Colour is expressed **only** through semantic tokens, with no literal colour value in any screen. Every surface is checked in both themes, including the ones easily missed: maps, delivery proof photos, the capture screen, empty states, the alarm screen and every error state. **The counter bill and order receipt PDFs stay light in both themes** — a shared, saved and sometimes printed document is not a screen, and the theme stops at the app's edge.

Formats are fixed: **Indian digit grouping** (1,00,000), **two decimals on every money amount without exception** because measured units produce genuine paise, **dd/mm/yyyy** dates, **12-hour time with am/pm**, and **Asia/Kolkata as the only display timezone with everything stored in UTC**. System font scaling is honoured to **130%** and tested there; nothing pins its own font size.

Accessibility commits to **AA text contrast and 48 x 48 dp minimum tap targets, in both themes**, verified during design and again during implementation. Systematic screen-reader and TalkBack testing, a full audit and any certification are **deferred, and stated as deferred**. **No claim of full WCAG 2.1 AA conformance is made anywhere without the verification that would support it**, whatever the reference PRD's wording implies.

## Roles and permissions

Two roles per shop: **Owner** and **Staff**.

Only the Owner can change online or counter prices, create or edit coupons, set bargain floor prices, see settlements, ledger, COD balance owed or revenue reports, add or deactivate staff, or change shop settings (opening hours, service radius, fulfilment modes, minimum order).

Staff can accept, partially accept or reject orders, set preparation time, mark ready, assign a deliverer, adjust stock and mark items out of stock, create counter bills, add products from the master catalog, and deliver when flagged. Staff see all shop orders plus a "My deliveries" list.

One staff account belongs to exactly one shop. The Owner invites staff by phone number, and the invited person completes the link by entering a one-time **join code** shown in the Owner's app. The code exists because a phone number is not verified until SMS sign-in arrives: without it, anyone who typed that number at sign-up would walk into the shop's account. Staff sign in the same way as everyone else. An account holds at most one active shop link at a time, as Owner or as Staff. The Owner deactivates rather than deletes, so order history keeps a real actor. There is no cap on staff count. Admin can view and suspend shop accounts and change any shop setting, but does not create staff.

**The staff-to-shop link is its own record carrying its own active flag, never a column on the account.** That single choice settles re-invitation. Re-inviting a deactivated phone number reactivates the existing link, so the person's past status transitions and counter bills keep pointing at the same actor — which is the whole reason deactivation exists instead of deletion. Only the Owner can do it; a deactivated person cannot re-enrol themselves. And a person deactivated at one shop may be invited by another: the first shop's history keeps its deactivated link while the second gets a fresh active one, which is only possible because the link is a record. Were it a column, that move would either corrupt the first shop's history or need a second account on the same phone, which the identity rule forbids. The one case nothing can undo is **staff deletion**, which removes the person's identity: a later invitation is a genuinely new account with no history, and the app says so rather than appearing to restore someone.

Admin web has a separate **Area Manager** role scoped to one Area.

## Fulfilment modes

Three modes: **Instant delivery**, **Scheduled delivery**, **Self-pickup**. Each shop toggles each mode on or off; Admin can force a mode off.

Scheduled delivery uses owner-defined slots, each with a maximum order capacity. Customers book an available slot for **same-day and next-day delivery**, with bookings allowed up to **one day ahead**. Next-day includes next-morning slots, so an order placed at night can take a slot the following morning. A slot at capacity disappears from the picker. A place in a slot is taken when the order is placed and given back if the order is rejected or cancelled; the count is checked at the moment of placement, so the last place cannot be sold twice. Bookings for a slot close at that slot's reject cutoff. Editing or removing a slot never cancels an order already booked into it.

One radius rule covers every mode: a shop is listed and orderable only when the customer is inside that shop's service radius, the customer's location being in an active Area to begin with (see Service area). There is no self-pickup exception for shops further away. Self-pickup carries no delivery fee.

**The platform maximum service radius is 5 km**, with each shop's own radius configurable below it at the 2-3 km the product describes. The maximum is not a product limit so much as a query bound: the nearby-shops search can only use its spatial index against a constant distance, so it filters on 5 km first and compares each shop's own radius afterwards. Raising the maximum is a one-line change and a slightly slower filter.

## Service area

Two layers. An **Area** is administrative: shop approval, reporting and pausing. **Serviceability** is distance from the shop, which is what "within 2-3 km" means in the product.

Each shop belongs to exactly one Area, derived from its pincode and editable by Admin. The customer feed is driven by distance. A customer whose location falls in no active Area sees "not serviceable yet" and can leave a phone number; those captures guide expansion.

**So two tests decide whether a customer can order from a shop, and both must pass.** First, the customer's location, by its pincode, is in an active Area. Second, that location is inside the shop's own service radius. The first test is about the customer's location alone and the second about distance alone, so the customer's Area and the shop's Area need not be the same one. The first test has a known cost, accepted when the service area was decided: pincodes are coarse, and a customer just inside a pincode that belongs to no active Area sees "not serviceable yet" even with a Live shop a few hundred metres away.

## Bargain

A toggle the Owner turns on per shop pack, with a hidden floor price for that pack. The floor is an absolute amount, never a percentage, and is never sent to a customer app. The customer offers a price for a chosen quantity, limited to three offers per item per day. **The system decides instantly** against the floor: at or above it accepts, below it rejects, with no shopkeeper in the loop. An accepted price is pinned to that quantity in the cart and expires in 30 minutes if the order is not placed; the pin holds a price, never stock. Rejected offers are recorded for the Owner. A bargained line takes no coupon or other discount, and a shop accepts it in full or removes it entirely. **An order that contains a bargained line takes no coupon at all**, whoever funds it; the customer removes either the coupon or the bargained line. The shop-wide discount still applies to that order's other lines. Bargain is online only, never in counter billing. Commission is computed on the accepted price.

Only a pack can be bargained; a quantity-price option cannot. The floor must be above zero and no higher than the pack's online price, and an offer at or above the online price simply buys at the online price. The three offers are counted per pack, per customer, per calendar day, accepted or not. A pinned price survives any later change to the price, the floor or the toggle for the rest of its 30 minutes. **Bargained lines count toward the minimum cart at their accepted price and are left out of every discount and coupon base.** The maximum effective discount percentage does not apply to a bargained price; the minimum commission guards it instead. Bargaining never changes whether a pickup order is viable, because pickup earns no commission on product value.

## Shop types

Grocery and kirana is the pilot focus. There is no second fixed shop type: every other type is an admin-defined category, so adding one needs no code change. Pharmacy and restaurants are out of scope, as is everything else in the excluded list below.

## Shop onboarding, excluded goods and suspension

A shop moves **Draft → Submitted → Under Review → Approved → Live → Suspended → Closed**, and **only Admin moves a shop to Live**. In stage one the founder holds both the Ops and the Admin hands, but the two records stay separate so the flow survives the arrival of Ops staff. **Live requires KYC complete, a recorded physical visit, and the onboarding fee paid or waived with a reason.** The visit is a hard precondition, stored as a geotagged shop-front photo with a timestamp and visit date; a shop can reach Approved without one but never Live. The Owner starts the application in the Shopkeeper app and Admin may correct any field. **The visit is also where the founder confirms that the phone number and email on the Owner's account belong to the person standing in the shop**, which in stage one is the only verification an Owner's phone number gets. The shop is shown its **rate card** before it goes Live, and the agreement it accepts records which version it saw.

Onboarding collects **PAN as a hard gate**, entity type (proprietorship, or registered firm, LLP or company), the **FSSAI number and its expiry date** — the number shown on the shop page, the date driving the auto-pause below — shop name, address with pincode and map pin, owner name, phone and email, service radius, and the shop's **UPI VPA**. **Identity documents are captured as numbers plus a verification record, never as document images** — PAN and FSSAI are collected and checked as numbers, and no scan or photograph of either is stored. Identity-document images for every shop sitting on one Single-AZ instance is the worst-shaped risk in the system and the cheapest one to decline at the outset; if something later genuinely requires an image, it is a decision to be made then and recorded. No bank details: the platform never pays the shop. **The VPA is not test-verified** — the customer app shows no QR, the shop's own staff show it at handover, so a typo surfaces in a day when the shop's money does not arrive, and no platform-side check could catch a staff member showing a personal QR anyway.

**The onboarding fee is waived for the entire pilot cohort**, as a recorded waiver with a reason rather than by removing the fee from the design. It is charged at stage two from the shops that continue. The pilot asks these shops to be unpaid testers, to hold a closed-test opt-in for fourteen days and to accept a commission invoice from an unregistered platform; a cash demand on top of that competes with the first statement, which is the collection that matters.

**Excluded in Phase 1, and nothing age-restricted at all**: prescription medicines and pharmacy of any kind, alcohol, tobacco, nicotine, pan masala and gutkha, prepared and restaurant food, raw meat, fish and poultry, live animals, fireworks, weapons and ammunition, pesticides and agro-chemicals, fuel, LPG and cylinders, lottery and gambling, adult products, and infant milk substitutes and feeding bottles. Excluding every age-restricted good is the point: there is no age gate, no ID capture and no deliverer judging anyone's age at a door. Non-prescription medicine stays out too.

**Enforcement is contractual, not technical.** The master catalog holds no excluded category, so nothing excluded can be ordered by a customer, and the onboarding agreement names the list with suspension as the consequence. A keyword denylist on counter-bill manual-line text **flags for Admin review and never blocks**, because blocking is bypassed by typing "item" and false-positives on "cigarette lighter". The manual line is an accepted hole. **A product request is a second one**: a requested product is sellable at the counter the moment it is requested, before anyone has looked at it, so an excluded good can sit in a shop's own counter list until Admin reviews the request. The same denylist therefore runs over product-request names and quantity-price option names, flagging and never blocking, and Admin rejects a request for an excluded good with that as the recorded reason. A product rejected for that reason is **removed from the shop's counter list from then on**: it can no longer be picked for a new bill or have stock added. Every bill, stock movement and report that already names it is kept as it was.

**Auto-pause** and **suspension** are distinct. Auto-pause is mechanical and clears when its cause does: expired FSSAI, an onboarding fee expired past its grace period, or the balance limit reached. Suspension is an Admin judgement with a recorded reason, on these grounds: selling an excluded category, diverting payment or disputing money a customer has paid, materially false listings or prices, upheld safety or expiry complaints, repeated unresponsiveness to accepted orders, non-payment of a statement past its grace period, a repayment to a customer left unpaid past its seven days, or KYC documents found invalid. A suspended shop leaves the customer feed and is not deleted, takes no new orders, and lets **in-flight orders run to completion**; **counter billing, inventory and catalog tools keep working**, because the till is the shop's own offline business. The outstanding statement stays due, reinstatement is Admin-only with a recorded reason, the owner sees the reason, and suspension attaches to **the shop and not the owner's account**.

## Search synonyms

An admin-managed global mapping of typed terms to catalog terms, applied to customer search only. Seeded with Hindi and transliterated terms for roughly the top 200 grocery items (kanda to onion, atta to flour). No automatic learning.

## Admin action record

An append-only record of **admin actions** is written from day one. There is no UI for it in Phase 1. Shop-side actions are not in it: they are stamped on the records they change instead, as order status history, stock movements and counter bills (see Order lifecycle).

## Order lifecycle

Seventeen statuses, eight of them terminal, listed with their transitions in #9. A terminal status is locked: later corrections are statement adjustments, never status edits. Deliverer assignment is a field on the order, not a status. `PICKED_UP` is distinct from `DELIVERED` because the two routes earn differently.

The shop is alerted at 120 seconds and the order auto-expires at 600 seconds, both configurable. Expiry charges nothing and fines nothing; it records a reliability event.

**Partial acceptance** reduces a quantity or removes a line. Never a substitution, never an addition. Reasons come from a fixed list. The server recomputes the whole price from the rule versions in the original snapshot. **Fees stand as quoted: a partial acceptance never adds or raises a fee**, and an order that now falls under the minimum cart is not rejected for it — the customer is shown the new total and why it changed, and decides. Partial acceptance is available at any point before the order is ready, not only at first acceptance, which the negative-stock and scheduled-order cases below both rely on; the customer's confirmation returns the order to the status it was in. **The customer must explicitly confirm a changed total within a configurable five-minute default, or ten minutes for a scheduled order; silence cancels the order and never counts as consent.** A partial acceptance may leave an order below the contribution floor. It then goes ahead as a **recorded exception, provided the platform's contribution is not negative**, because the shortage was the shop's and not the customer's. **A platform-funded discount never exceeds what the platform earns on the order**: on the new total it is reduced or removed to keep that true, and the customer sees it with the reason. If the contribution would still be negative, the order is cancelled at no cost to the customer.

Cancellation is free for the customer before acceptance, needs the shop's consent between acceptance and ready, and is impossible in the app once the order is ready. The shop may cancel after acceptance with a reason. Admin may force any transition with a reason.

A failed delivery is one attempt and terminal, after at least one call attempt, a configured wait, a photo and a reason from a fixed list. Goods return to on-hand stock, not merely unreserved. An uncollected pickup order expires at a configurable cutoff with no booking fee charged to anyone.

A scheduled order is auto-approved at placement and the shop gets a review task; it may reject until a configurable cutoff before the slot starts, and inaction leaves the order confirmed. **That automatic approval is the order's acceptance**: stock is reserved, the place in the slot is taken and the handover code is generated at that moment, so a scheduled order is refused at placement if the stock or the slot is not there. The acceptance alert and expiry timers do not apply to it. After the cutoff the shop can no longer reject; it can only cancel with a reason, which records a reliability event. Stock reserved overnight may be sold at the counter by morning, and the shop then partially accepts the scheduled order like any other. Slots stay owner-defined and capacity-capped. **The customer may cancel a scheduled order freely until that same cutoff**, which is shown on the slot and on the order; from then until the order is ready the shop must agree, and after that it cannot be cancelled in the app. A changed total on a scheduled order must be confirmed within **ten minutes**, set separately from the five of other orders; silence cancels the order and gives back its stock and its place in the slot.

**Stock is reserved at acceptance**, never in the cart and never while an order waits for the shop, and released on every terminal state except delivery and pickup, which convert it to a decrement. Online orders and counter bills share one quantity, held as an append-only movement log. **A counter bill is never blocked by a reservation**: it decrements freely, available stock may go negative, and the shop gets an alert naming the affected online orders.

Every status change records actor, role, timestamp, reason code and the client id of any offline-queued action. The same stamp goes on stock movements and counter bills, which is the shop's accountability trail; there is no separate audit surface in Phase 1.

## Notifications

**Two channels in Phase 1: a push notification and an in-app list. No transactional email except the sign-in code, and no SMS at all**, since SMS waits on DLT registration and therefore on the entity. Two other emails exist and are the same kind of thing, a message about access to the account itself and not about an order: the code that confirms a request made on the public deletion page, and the notice sent to the old address when Admin changes an account's email.

**A push is only ever a mirror of a state change already recorded, and a missed push never changes an outcome.** This has to hold: the Shopkeeper app works offline and phones drop notifications routinely, so no transition, charge or expiry may depend on one being delivered.

A customer is notified when an order is accepted, partially accepted, rejected, expired, out for delivery, ready for pickup, delivered, or cancelled by the shop, when a complaint is resolved, when a repayment is recorded as owed to them, and when the shop marks it paid. A shop is notified of a new order, a new scheduled order, the customer's confirmation or refusal of a changed total, a customer cancellation, negative stock caused by a counter bill, the balance reaching 80 per cent and then the limit, suspension and reinstatement, a product request approved or rejected, a shop-wide discount or a coupon it funds paused because the cap was lowered, a change to its rate card and the week it starts, a complaint upheld against it, and a repayment recorded against it. A deliverer is notified when assigned a delivery. **Admin receives no push at all** — Admin web times out after 30 minutes idle, so every admin item is a queue it works rather than an alert it chases.

**Exactly two notifications are loud.** The new-order push carries sound and high priority because the order auto-expires at 600 seconds. The changed-total confirmation is both a push and a persistent blocking banner in the customer app, because silence cancels the order — it is the only notification whose absence costs a customer their order. **Low stock stays an in-app list and is never a push**, as set out above.

## Complaints

A customer raises a complaint against a **delivered or picked-up** order from that order's screen, choosing a **fixed category** with an optional photo. The categories are missing item, wrong item, damaged, expired or unsafe, overcharged, behaviour, and other. **A complaint may be raised until the end of the third calendar day after the delivery or pickup.** After that the app points to the support email, and Admin may open a late complaint for a serious safety issue, recording why. The customer sees at once that it was received. It lands in an Admin queue. The shop is not told until a complaint is upheld, and because it has no reply in the app, Admin speaks to the shop outside the app before upholding anything that costs it money. Admin resolves it with a recorded reason and, where money is owed, a manual statement adjustment — the existing mechanism, since there is no fines engine. **Upheld safety and expiry complaints are what feed the suspension ground of the same name**, which is why the mechanism cannot be left undefined even though it needs no decision ticket of its own.

Out of Phase 1: any service-level commitment, any chat thread, any shop-side reply, and any automatic consequence. Every outcome passes through an Admin judgement.

## Ratings

After an order is delivered or picked up the customer is asked for a **1 to 5 star rating of the shop, and nothing else: no written comment**. Skipping is allowed. Unmoderated public text about a named local shop is a risk with nobody to handle it, and a complaint already carries the detail to Admin. A shop's rating is the **average over a rolling 90 days, shown only once it has three ratings**; both numbers are configurable. Ratings appear on the shop card and shop page; they are not on the shop's own report screens in Phase 1.

## Customer refusals and blocking

Door refusals and failed deliveries are counted per customer. **Two within 30 days flags the customer in an Admin queue**, both figures configurable. **Admin may block or unblock a customer with a recorded reason, and nothing blocks automatically.** A blocked customer cannot place a new order, sees that the account is blocked with the support email, and lets any in-flight order finish. Because a deleted phone number may sign up again at once, a block is meant to outlive account deletion, or deleting the account would be the way round it; so **while a block is active a keyed hash of the phone number and of the email is kept**, which cannot be read back, and a new sign-up matching either starts blocked. Unblocking destroys both, and nothing is kept for a customer who is not blocked. It is the one thing that outlives the erasure, and the privacy policy says so. **In stage one a block is a deterrent and not a wall**, because the phone number is not verified and a determined person can return with another number and another email.

## Support and contact

**The platform's only support channel is an email address on the brand domain**, shown in both apps and on the public support page. There is no platform phone or WhatsApp number in Phase 1. **For a live order the customer calls the shop**: the order detail always carries the shop's phone number as a call button, because the shop is the party that can fix the order. The assigned deliverer sees the customer's phone number for that order only, which the failed-delivery rule requires. There is no masked calling.

## Catalog

Admin owns **master products**, their base unit and their canonical **packs**; a shop lists packs and sets its own price, counter price, MRP, stock, availability, low-stock threshold, bargain toggle and floor. A shop cannot create a free-text product that sells online: it submits a request, which is sellable at the counter immediately and hidden from the customer feed until approved.

A shop may also define its own **quantity-price options** on its listing: name, measurement unit, quantity and a total price for that quantity, several per listing ("2 pav for Rs 6", "12 pav for Rs 34"). This is how a quantity discount is expressed, instead of duplicate products. Every option carries a quantity in the master's base unit, so price per base unit stays computable across shops, and **stock is tracked in the base unit only** — an option deducts its quantity, and no option holds stock of its own.

Units are **`each`** (count) or **`measured`** (price per unit with a shop-set minimum and step, so a line is always a count of steps). **Money precision and quantity precision are separate concerns**: a money amount carries two decimals and a quantity carries three, because 0.250 kg is a real quantity while a third decimal of a rupee is not a real price. Quantity times unit price therefore produces more decimals than money has, and that collapses **once per line, rounded half-up to two decimals, with the rounded line total stored rather than recomputed** — which is the difference between a bill that adds up and an argument at a counter. **There is no reweighing at packing**: the selected quantity and price are fixed at checkout and written into the immutable placement snapshot, which is what settlement aggregates. A shop that cannot pack to the selected quantity uses partial acceptance.

Two category levels, the second optional. **Categories and master products are one global list.** Per-Area availability of a category is deferred, as the launch-shape and service-area decisions both recorded, and with one Area in stage one it would change nothing. When it arrives it acts on the shop's Area, since a customer belongs to none. Seed categories are Groceries, Fruits and Vegetables, Stationery. The print and xerox service is out of Phase 1 and will be reconsidered only if time allows.

In Phase 1: a plain expiry date per listing with no automatic pricing behaviour, a shop-wide discount (shop-funded, in the two forms set out in Pricing engine below), barcode scanning against existing EAN or UPC codes only, and an admin CSV importer. Out: near-expiry automatic discounts, shop-side bulk upload, multiple images, branches, label printing. A counter bill may carry a flagged **manual line** that touches no stock and offers one tap to create a pending product request.

## Counter billing and offline

A **counter bill** records catalog lines or a manual line, a bill-level discount, an optional customer name and phone, an optional **"customer agreed to receive offers from YGo"** consent tick stamped with the bill, date and shop, and a payment mode of **cash or UPI only**. Output is a PDF through the phone's share sheet, carrying a YGo download QR and short link; there is no printer. There is no unpaid mode and no customer credit ledger. Owner and Staff may bill; **only the Owner voids a bill**, within a configurable 24 hours, with a reason, as a new movement that returns stock and never edits the original. There is no partial return and no line edit. A manual line's text is checked against a small admin denylist that **flags the bill for review and never blocks it**.

Offline: **counter billing, stock adjustment, marking out of stock, and the delivery screens**. Online only: accepting, rejecting or partially accepting an order, marking ready, price and catalog edits, and anything about money owed. The device caches its own listings, stock and today's accepted orders, queues up to a configurable 500 actions or 72 hours, and then blocks new billing until it syncs.

Conflict rules: **orders and money records, the server wins**. A counter bill is a movement, not a value, so it applies as a delta to the log in any order it arrives. Only an absolute *set stock to N* can conflict, and those resolve by **server receipt order, never the client clock**; a device whose clock is outside a configurable skew stamps its queued actions as clock-suspect.

Stock is corrected per product with a **required reason code** (received, counted, damaged, expired, returned, correction); there is no stock-take screen. At zero or below a listing is **visible but not orderable**, never hidden, and a manual out-of-stock toggle stays independent of the count. Low stock is an in-app list, never a push. Negative stock offers two taps per affected order, partially accept it or fix the count, and **no order is ever auto-cancelled by a stock event**.

Every counter bill carries two identifiers. The **bill reference** is made on the phone at the moment of sale, is unique and permanent, and appears on every copy. The **bill number** is **sequential per shop, unique, and never reused or skipped**, and is given by the server when the bill reaches it, so no two bills in a shop can share one. Numbers follow the order in which bills reach the server, which with two phones offline is not always the order of sale; each bill keeps its own sale time. A bill shared before it has synced shows its reference and says its number is pending; shared again afterwards it shows both, and either one finds the bill. A bill held back for attention has a reference and no number until it is resolved, which is why the sequence never has a gap. Sending the same bill twice returns the same number. A voided bill keeps both; the void is a separate record.

**The total a counter bill showed at the counter is the total on record.** The server checks the arithmetic when the bill arrives, and a disagreement flags the bill for Admin and changes nothing, because the customer has already paid.

Shop reports in Phase 1: sales today and this week with the online and counter split, top items, the balance statement, and rejected bargain offers.

## Accounts and sign-in

**Every role signs in with Google Sign-In, or an email OTP as the fallback.** On iOS, Sign in with Apple is offered beside Google, because Apple requires it of any app that offers Google. No passwords. The **phone number is still the account identity**: signup collects phone and email, the code goes to the email, and the account is keyed by phone, so switching to SMS later is a configuration change and not a migration. Email is unique per account. **One account per phone number, carrying a set of roles**, with an app-level switch between the Customer and Shopkeeper surfaces; an Owner or Staff member may not place a customer order with their own shop.

**An account is created at first sign-in.** Signing in proves the email; the app then asks for the phone number before anything else can be done. **In stage one that phone number is claimed, not verified**, and the account records it as unverified. A number is always in one of three states, which Admin can see and which are never treated as the same thing: **unverified**, **visit-confirmed** for an Owner whose shop has had its visit, and **SMS-verified** once SMS sign-in exists. Three things follow and are stated so nobody relies on more than is there. A number already in use is refused, and the person is pointed to the support email, since someone may have typed it by mistake or on purpose. Nothing that grants access to a shop rests on a phone number alone: the Owner role is confirmed by Admin at the in-person visit, and a Staff link needs the Owner's join code. And a customer can order under a number that is not theirs, which shows up only when the deliverer's call fails. **The owner has accepted that for stage one only.** An unverified number never proves on its own who someone is and never moves an account or its history to another person, and the acceptance lapses at the stage-two review. If SMS is not ready by then, whether it continues is decided at that review and recorded; the app carries no default, and Admin can stop new customer sign-ups with a switch. When SMS is switched on, every account, a visit-confirmed Owner's included, confirms its number by SMS at next sign-in, and a number its holder cannot confirm is released.

**Every account starts as a Customer.** The Owner role is attached when the account starts a shop application and means nothing until Admin moves that shop to Live. The Staff role is attached when an invited person enters the join code. Admin and Area Manager accounts are created by an Admin and are never open to sign-up.

Phone with an SMS OTP is the end state, not the soft-launch state: DLT registration needs a registered business, and the entity is registered only after the soft launch. SMS is therefore **built now behind a configuration flag** — one channel interface, two adapters, nothing above it knowing which channel sent the code — and the DLT header and template text is drafted during the soft launch.

The login OTP is 6 digits, valid 5 minutes, 5 wrong attempts then a 15-minute lock, 5 requests an hour per phone. It is generated with a CSPRNG, stored hashed, and **never logged and never in an API response**. Admin web times out after 30 minutes idle; mobile sessions persist 90 days by refresh. The access token lives in memory and the refresh token in the OS secure store, or an httpOnly cookie on web, **never in `localStorage`**; refresh tokens rotate on use and are revocable, so a deactivated Staff member loses access within minutes.

The **handover OTP** is 4 digits, generated at acceptance and carried with the order, revealed in the customer app at Items OK or when the check timer ends, 3 attempts then a cooldown and 6 in total. Offline it verifies against a per-order salted hash, and **the server re-validates every queued delivery on sync**, flagging a mismatch for Admin. The same OTP completes a self-pickup at the counter, without the timer or photo. The only fallback is **a photo plus a required reason**, which delivers the order but flags it as a **proof exception**, counted per shop and per deliverer.

**Account recovery when someone loses access to the email on file is an Admin email change with a recorded reason, and there is no self-service path until SMS is live.** The verification bar differs by what the account controls. For a **customer**, a call alone proves nothing while the phone number is unverified, so Admin calls the phone on file **and** the caller gives **two details from the account's own order history**, such as a recent order's shop, date and total; a notice goes to the old email address; the email is then changed with the checks and the reason recorded, and **every session is revoked**. An account with no completed order has nothing to check against and is not recovered: Admin releases its phone number and the person signs up again. For a shop **Owner**, whose account controls money, the bar is a match against the PAN or FSSAI on file or the recorded geotagged visit. This is an account-takeover path whose only defence is a human who personally knows the pilot cohort; that defence is real at a few dozen shops and does not survive growth, so it is recorded as a **known weakness that SMS OTP retires in stage two**, not as a control.

Because the soft launch ships as a public Play release, **Play review is on the critical path**: the reviewer demo account, now keyed to an email address, in-app account deletion with the publicly reachable deletion-request page beside it, a published privacy policy and a completed Data safety declaration. DLT and SMS-provider onboarding is a blocker for the official launch, not the soft launch.

## Account deletion and data retention

A **Customer deletes their own account** from Settings, in two taps, with no reason required. An **Owner or Staff member requests deletion in the app and Admin processes it**; an Owner is blocked while the shop is Live or a statement is outstanding, and the app names which of the two is blocking. Staff deletion removes the person's identity and leaves the deactivated actor on their past transitions and bills.

**Deletion is blocked while any order is in a non-terminal status**, with that stated as the reason; where cancellation is still allowed the flow offers cancel-then-delete. There is no scheduled deletion that fires later.

Deletion happens in two steps, because a restore and an erasure cannot both be immediate. **On the day of deletion the account is closed**: sessions are revoked and refresh tokens invalidated, the person cannot sign in, shops see "Deleted customer" at once, and the personal details are sealed where nothing but an Admin restore can reach them. There is **no self-service undo**. **For 7 days Admin may restore the account on request**, with its addresses and order history; carts, bargain pins and push tokens are not brought back. **When the 7 days end, the personal details are erased and the order rows anonymised, and nothing can reverse it.** Everything is complete **within 30 days of the request**, which is the promise the privacy policy makes; for a request Admin processes — an Owner, a Staff member or the public page — the 7 days start when Admin acts, so Admin must act within 23. Copies of the data inside backups age out within 90 days, and if a backup is ever restored, the deletions made since are applied again before anyone uses it. **Hard-deleted when the restore window closes**: phone, email, name, saved addresses and map pins, push tokens, waitlist captures, search history, bargain offer history, delivery proof photos and handover OTP hashes. **Anonymised in place**: order rows, keeping the order id, the placement snapshot's lines and amounts, the shop, the timestamps and the commission basis against a **stable opaque pseudonym**. Those anonymised financial records are retained **eight years**, covering both the income-tax and GST windows.

A **deleted phone number may sign up again immediately**, as a new account with no history; anonymised orders never reattach. **Signing up again inside the 7 days gives up the restore**, because one phone number or email cannot belong to two accounts: the old account then runs on to erasure and cannot be brought back. The shop keeps every past order, with the customer shown as **"Deleted customer"**, name, phone and full address removed, and only the Area and distance band kept for reporting.

The walk-in name and phone on a counter bill belongs to someone who usually holds no account, so an account deletion never touches it. It is kept as the shop's own record; **only numbers carrying the consent tick are ever marketable**, and **no platform messaging reaches them before stage two**, when it goes out DLT-registered, DND-scrubbed and consent-only with an opt-out. The consent tick is built in Phase 1 because consent cannot be obtained retroactively.

### Shop data, and what is mandatory as against optional

The eight-year window above is **the platform's own statutory obligation, not the customer's**, so it applies identically to shop-side records — anchored to the **last financial record** rather than to the date a shop closed. A Closed shop is never purged while a statement, invoice or commission record exists: it leaves the customer feed, its listings stop selling and counter billing stops, and anything outstanding stays due. Three classes, each with its own clock, because treating them alike would either destroy evidence or hoard data:

- **Mandatory, kept eight years from the last financial record.** Commission invoices, weekly statements, ledger entries and adjustments. The immutable placement snapshot's lines, amounts and commission basis, with the order id, the shop and the timestamps. And the shop's **invoice identity** — legal name, address, entity type, PAN number, FSSAI number — because an invoice must identify its counterparty. GST records join this class at stage two.
- **Business record, kept for the shop but not statutory for the platform.** Counter bills and the stock movement log. The platform earns no commission on them, so they evidence nothing it invoiced, but purging them destroys the shop's own books; they are kept on the same eight-year clock. The **record** that a physical visit happened, with its date and actor, belongs here too, as the evidence behind the Live gate.
- **Removable once eligible.** The Owner's and Staff's personal contact details — name, phone, email, push tokens — hard-deleted on account deletion exactly as a customer's are. The **UPI VPA**, which appears on no invoice. The **geotagged shop-front photograph**, purged one year after closure while the visit record itself stays. Rejected negotiated offers, search history, and device and sync metadata including clock-suspect flags.

### Deletion exceptions, stated together

The cases where deletion does not happen, or does not happen yet. Three are set out above and repeated here only as a list; the rest are specific to shops:

- An **Owner cannot delete** while the shop is Live or a statement is outstanding, and the app names which of the two is blocking. **Nobody can delete** while any order is in a non-terminal status.
- **Financial records are anonymised, never deleted**, for the full eight years. Deletion removes identity, not history.
- **An Owner's deletion removes the Owner's personal data but never the shop's invoice identity** inside that window. This is the exception most likely to surprise: the person goes, the counterparty on invoices already issued stays, because an invoice that cannot name who it was issued to is not evidence of anything.
- **Staff deletion** leaves the deactivated actor on past transitions and bills, and **is not reversible by re-invitation** — a later invitation is a new account with no history.
- The **walk-in name and phone on a counter bill** is untouched by any account deletion; it is the shop's own record of someone who holds no account.
- An **Admin restore is available for 7 days** unless the phone number or email has been used for a new account in the meantime, with erasure and anonymisation when the 7 days end, everything complete within 30 days of the request, and no self-service undo at any point. A **deleted phone number may sign up again immediately** as a new account, and anonymised orders never reattach to it.

A **public deletion-request page** on the marketing domain takes a phone number, the email on file and the request type, **deletes nothing directly**, verifies by emailing a code, and feeds an Admin queue worked inside the 30-day window. A number with no account gets the same neutral response, so the page cannot be used to discover who is a customer, and it accepts a free-text request from someone with no account at all. It must exist **before Play review**.

## Launch stages and tax posture

The launch has **two stages**. Stage one is a **soft launch of one to two weeks with no registered entity**: real shops, real customers, real orders, real money, email sign-in, and commission charged. Stage two follows if it works — entity and GST registration, then SMS OTP, the platform's own FSSAI licence, and the statutory outputs below.

**Stage one is 12 to 20 shops in one Area.** The system is sized for 50 to 80 shops, which is the stage-two target and not the pilot. Every shop needs the founder's visit in person and its products in the seed catalog, so the smaller number is what one person can onboard properly.

**Stage two is decided at the end of week two, on four measures and no revenue target**: at least 80 per cent of placed orders delivered or picked up; at least half of all placed instant and pickup orders accepted by the shop within two minutes of placement, counting an order the shop rejects or lets expire as not accepted in time, and leaving scheduled orders out because they are approved automatically; at least 80 per cent of shops paying their first weekly statement within the grace period; and no money dispute caused by the app's arithmetic. The soft launch ends at two weeks with a decision to continue, fix or stop. It does not drift on, because each extra week as an unregistered platform adds exposure for less new information.

**If time runs short, features are cut in this order**: Area Manager accounts and payouts, since the founder is the only operator in stage one; split-funded coupons, keeping shop-funded and platform-funded ones; pickup booking fees, which ship disabled; Scheduled delivery; and last the iOS release, which may trail Android. **Never cut**: the order journey, pricing and the price simulator, offline counter billing, delivery proof, weekly statements, Bargain, ratings and account deletion.

Stage one ships as a **public Google Play release**, so the Play-review items above are launch blockers. A personal Play account created after 13 November 2023 cannot reach production until a closed test has run with **at least twelve testers continuously opted in for fourteen days**; the pilot shops are that cohort, tester opt-in is an onboarding step, and the count dropping below twelve restarts the clock.

**Customer-facing prices are tax-inclusive with no tax breakup.** No HSN sits on a master product and no line-level tax is displayed or computed. A shop's prices are already inclusive, and a breakup nobody can validate across hundreds of products would be a fabricated number on a customer's receipt.

**The shop is the seller of record and the platform is never a seller of goods.** Both customer documents — the online order receipt and the counter bill — are the shop's own sale, issued in the shop's name and address and carrying the shop's GSTIN only if it has one. The platform generates the online receipt; that does not make it the platform's sale. **The customer's bill carries nothing of the platform's** — no platform charge, no platform tax line, no commission. Commission exists only between the platform and the shop, on the weekly statement, and is accounted for separately from the shop's sales. This holds only while every rupee the customer pays is the shop's: disclosing any fee to a customer **as a YGo charge** would make it the platform's own supply at 18%, so Phase 1 has no such fee.

An order receipt shows the shop's identity, the lines from the order's immutable placement snapshot with quantity and price, shop-funded discounts and any accepted bargain price, the delivery fee and any disclosed small-cart or booking fee, the total, the collected payment method, and the order id and timestamp — every amount tax-inclusive, with no tax line.

Commission is charged from day one, but in stage one the weekly statement is a **commercial invoice and not a tax invoice**: no GST line, no GSTIN, and it says so on its face, because a shop that reads it as a tax invoice will claim input credit that does not exist. **No input tax credit is available to any shop on commission during stage one**, which belongs in the onboarding conversation rather than being discovered later. Every statutory output — the commission tax invoice, the GST line, TCS and TDS — sits behind a single **registered-entity flag** that flips at stage two. Nothing statutory is removed from the design; it is switched off.

PAN and entity type are collected at onboarding for a statutory reason, not a bureaucratic one. Section 194O makes the platform responsible for deducting 0.1% of facilitated sales even though the shop collects the money directly, so the exemption matters: an individual or HUF shop under Rs 5 lakh of platform sales in a financial year with PAN furnished is exempt, while a firm, LLP or company has no threshold at all. **Stage one therefore prefers proprietorship shops**; a non-proprietor shop needs a TAN in place first. The rest of the approval set is settled above in Shop onboarding, excluded goods and suspension.

Every statutory figure here needs a chartered accountant's confirmation before stage two, and again after each GST Council or Finance Act change. See `docs/research/financial-architecture-review.md`.

## Money

Customers pay by **cash or UPI at the handover**: at the door for a delivery, to the deliverer, who is the shop's own staff, and **at the shop's counter for a pickup**, when the order is collected. Nothing is paid in advance on any route, so a pickup order that is never collected costs nobody anything. UPI uses the shop's own VPA: **the customer app never shows a QR** — the deliverer shows one from the Shopkeeper app at handover — so the money lands in the shop's account and the platform never touches customer funds. There is no online prepaid gateway in Phase 1. Staff record the collected method per order, for a pickup as for a delivery. A pickup booking fee, where one is switched on, is part of the total the shop collects and is owed to the platform on the statement.

**The shop collects and the shop owes.** Every rupee the customer pays reaches the shop. The platform invoices what it earned and the shop pays the net on a weekly statement. "Shop payout" means the amount the shop retains, never a transfer from the platform. A period that nets in the shop's favour is settled by an authorised manual adjustment and an offline transfer.

Unpaid platform earnings accrue as a balance visible to the shop and to Admin, warning at 80% of the shop's limit and auto-pausing new orders at 100%. The limit is unset and auto-pause is off until an admin sets it.

Settlement runs weekly on Monday for the preceding Monday-to-Sunday period, aggregating an **immutable pricing snapshot** written at each order's placement. Adjustments are new ledger entries; a snapshot is never rewritten. The shop pays manually and an admin records the payment.

Because nothing is prepaid and UPI is collected at handover, **there is no refund flow**. Cancellation is free before acceptance and allowed until the shop marks the order ready. A refusal at the door marks the order undelivered and charges no commission. Anything owed after delivery is an admin adjustment on the next statement, with the shop returning cash directly. **The adjustment moves money between the shop and the platform only; it never pays the customer.** The platform holds no customer money and has no means of paying one, so a customer is made whole only by the shop. **When a complaint is upheld and money is owed, Admin records the amount and the shop pays the customer directly within seven calendar days.** Both see the amount, the due date and the status: owed, marked paid by the shop, confirmed by the customer, or disputed. A dispute or a missed date goes to an Admin queue. **Nothing happens to the shop automatically.** A repayment left unpaid past its date is a ground on which Admin may suspend the shop, and that suspension is the platform's one lever.

Area Managers are paid a configurable share of the commission **actually collected** in their Area, in the weekly run but as a separate platform expense, never netted into a shop's statement.

## Pricing engine

`docs/product/revenue-system-spec.md` is authoritative for pricing and settlement mechanics and governs where it and a mockup disagree. In Phase 1 the data model is complete while the admin UI is reduced to global, category and shop scopes.

Commission is computed per line on delivered orders, with the configured default base being eligible product value after shop-funded discounts and after any accepted bargain price, before platform-funded promotion value, excluding fees and taxes. **Pickup earns no product commission** — only an optional disclosed customer booking fee and an optional fixed shop booking fee, both on completed pickup, both shipping disabled, under pickup's own contribution rule. **Counter bills earn no commission** and carry no per-bill charge during the pilot, because charging per bill would give a shop reason to omit sales and corrupt the inventory the feature exists to keep accurate; a flat subscription is the preferred later alternative.

The contribution floor is the ordinary viability guard, with a configurable minimum commission governing Bargain orders so an accepted offer is always honoured. When an order is not viable the published order is: reject an ineligible platform-funded promotion, apply only eligible disclosed fees within caps, offer a larger basket or pickup, then disable delivery checkout with a clear reason. Any exception below the floor is explicitly authorised and recorded with a reason. The owner has given one standing authorisation: an order that a partial acceptance leaves below the floor, set out under Partial acceptance above.

Delivery fees carry configurable platform and shop shares. Both a minimum cart and a small-cart fee exist, disclosed before checkout. Coupons are funded by the platform, the shop or an explicit split, one per order, with platform funding capped at the contribution surplus above the floor. **At most one shop-funded offer applies to an order**: where a shop-funded coupon and the shop-wide discount both qualify, the one that takes more off that order applies, the shop-wide discount on a tie, and a coupon that was not applied is not counted as used. A split-funded coupon counts as a shop-funded offer for this rule. A platform-funded coupon keeps its own rule and may sit on a shop-wide discount. The maximum effective shop-funded discount percentage binds a coupon an Owner creates, and the shop-funded share of a split-funded one, exactly as it binds the shop-wide discount. There is no fines engine; Admin applies manual statement adjustments instead.

The **shop-wide discount** is shop-funded, Owner-configured, and takes **two forms and no other**: a **percentage with an optional maximum amount** ("10 per cent off up to 20 rupees"), or a **flat amount with a required minimum order value** ("200 rupees off on orders of 1,000 rupees or more"). The minimum order value is required on the flat form because a flat amount without one has no bounded effective percentage. A configurable **maximum effective shop-funded discount percentage**, defaulting to **20 per cent** with per-shop overrides, bounds both; it is enforced when the rule is published, on each form's worst case, so an invalid rule cannot exist. At most one shop-wide discount applies to an order, so percentages never compound. Lowering the maximum **pauses** the rules that now breach it and tells their Owner, rather than quietly reducing a discount a customer was shown. The discount base is pre-discount eligible product value, never fees and never taxes, and a negotiated line takes no discount at all.

**Shop-funded and platform-funded discounts stay separate amounts at every stage** — quote, ledger and placement snapshot — each carrying its rule id and version, with no single discount field anywhere. This is load-bearing: the commission base is eligible product value after shop-funded discounts and before platform-funded promotion value, so merging them would shrink the base by the platform's own promotional spend and the platform would under-bill itself. An order-level shop-funded discount is apportioned across eligible lines pro rata in paise, remainder to the largest line, because commission is computed per line.

**This is platform-loss prevention, not shop-profit protection, and the difference is never blurred.** The cap guards against a misconfiguration and against a discount collapsing the commission base; the contribution floor guards the platform's contribution. **Nothing claims a shop is selling profitably**, because the platform holds no cost-of-goods data and no selling-price floor — MRP is a manufacturer's ceiling, not a cost — so the engine cannot distinguish a shop discounting its margin from one selling below what it paid, and no surface implies it can. Real shop-profit protection would need a per-pack cost price and a selling-price floor derived from it, which depends on shops disclosing purchase prices they have good reason to withhold; that is why it is out of Phase 1, as a data dependency rather than an effort estimate.

**No rate, fee, floor, split, limit or booking fee is hardcoded.** An admin must configure the required values before a shop can receive orders; a shop with incomplete pricing configuration stays out of the customer feed and cannot accept orders, while its counter billing, inventory and catalog tools keep working.

**The first rate card is set before the first shop goes Live, not after real orders.** "Unset until real baskets have been seen" cannot mean live baskets, since a shop without a rate card takes no orders and would never produce one. The first values are chosen in the price simulator against baskets built from the pilot shops' own price lists, gathered on the onboarding visits, and are then revised from real orders during the pilot. **A revision to a Live shop's rate card is told to the shop in the app and in person, takes effect from the start of the next statement week, and is never applied backwards**; every placed order keeps the values it was placed under, and a mistake found mid-week is put right by a statement adjustment. A value left empty is not configured, while a zero that was deliberately entered is. The values a shop cannot go Live without are: the commission rule, the delivery fee and its shares, the delivered-order contribution floor, the minimum cart, the statement grace period, and — only where the shop uses them — the pickup contribution rule and the Bargain minimum commission. The balance limit is not among them, because its pause stays off until a limit is set. Values set once at the global level cover every shop that has no override of its own.

**There is one authoritative calculation and it runs on the server.** The customer's cart, the placed order, a recomputation after partial acceptance, the price simulator and the weekly statement all use it, and a customer's phone only ever displays what the server quoted. The one money figure worked out on a phone is a counter bill's total, because that is the one sale made without a connection.

## Downstream obligations

These are unresolved and belong to named tickets. Do not invent rules for them here.

- **Offline sync transport, idempotency and replay**: Offline sync approach (#20). The policy — what works offline, the queue limits and the conflict rules — is settled in Counter billing and offline above.
- **The SMS or WhatsApp OTP provider**: not chosen. Its onboarding blocks the official launch, not the soft launch.
- **Amazon SES production access**, in the Mumbai region. **This is a soft-launch blocker and was not previously listed as one.** Every sign-in code goes by email until the entity is registered, and a new SES account is sandboxed per region: it can send only to addresses it has already verified, capped at 200 messages a day. Until production access is granted, no shopkeeper and no customer can receive a login code. Approval is reported to take about a day and a verified sending domain helps, so it is cheap — but only if it is started before launch week rather than during it.
- **The six-month AWS account clock.** A new AWS account gets a credit-based Free plan of 100 US dollars on sign-up and up to 100 more for completing set-up tasks. At six months, or when the credits run out, **AWS suspends the account, holds the data for ninety days, and then erases it permanently**; the plan cannot be extended. Unused credits carry over on upgrade, so upgrading early costs nothing and forgetting costs the account. The rule is settled (ADR-0005): **development stays on the Free plan, and the account is upgraded before any real shop or customer data is stored or the pilot goes live, whichever comes first.** What remains outstanding is the pair of reminders, set the day the account is opened — one for credit exhaustion, one for the six-month date — and a payment instrument that can take a recurring rupee charge, which the RBI e-mandate rules make a real failure mode rather than a formality.
- **Which services the Free plan blocks: unverifiable in advance.** Amazon's own guidance says a Free plan account is limited from reaching a subset of services and never says which. So the Free-plan-during-development rule is confirmed only by hitting a wall, and any refusal is the signal to upgrade immediately rather than something to design around. The likeliest wall is Amazon SES production access, which is on the launch path.
- **Whether AWS credits reduce the GST base: unresolved, and it decides what is payable in cash from month one.** The running cost is approved at a provisional 4,000 to 4,500 rupees a month and the verified figure is about 44 US dollars including 18 per cent GST (ADR-0005). No Amazon document says whether a credit is applied before or after tax, so the plan assumes the worse case — **credits cover service charges and the GST is paid in cash, about 590 rupees a month during the credit period**. The first real invoice settles it. GST stays unclaimable until the entity is GST-registered in stage two.
- **A five-page public website**, discovered while resolving the web surfaces (ADR-0006) and previously owned by nobody: a landing page, a privacy policy, terms, the #26 account-deletion request page and a support contact page. It is **not marketing polish for later**. Google Play requires a public privacy policy URL, #26 requires the deletion-request page before Play review, and Amazon SES production access is commonly refused when the sending domain has no real site behind it — which makes this a dependency of email sign-in and therefore of the soft launch. It must be live on the real domain well before launch week.
- **A domain name and the brand spelling**: researched, **deferred by the owner on 13 Oct 2026** because the company name is not settled, and tracked in #27. It is needed for the sending domain that makes email sign-in deliverable, for the TLS certificate and for the web apps, and it is the one launch cost **AWS credits do not cover** — roughly 700 to 1,300 rupees a year on renewal, where the renewal price rather than the heavily discounted first year is the figure to compare. Every short form of the name is already registered; ADR-0005 lists what is available and recommends `ygonow.in`. The spelling itself is unresolved: this repository is `YGo` and the owner has also written "YUGO", and customers see that difference in the app listing name, the sender address and the domain. **This is now the longest lead-time item on the launch path and it sets the earliest possible pilot date.** Once a name exists, four things must happen in sequence — purchase and DNS delegation, then DKIM, SPF and DMARC, then the five-page public site live on it, then SES production access for `ap-south-1`, which is discretionary, takes days and can fail once. That chain is about **two to three weeks** and cannot be compressed, and until it completes no shopkeeper and no customer can receive a sign-in code. The build is unaffected and proceeds in parallel.
- **The Apple individual developer enrolment**: not started. It is the only platform item that cannot be solved by writing code, it has been reported to fail at the payment step from India, and it gates the iOS submission rather than the soft launch.
- **Four toolchain facts the mobile stack assumes and nobody has verified**, each recorded in ADR-0001: that Expo SDK 57 targets Android API 36, which Play requires of new apps and which first bites at the soft launch submission; that Xcode personal-team provisioning can install a build on a real iPhone without the paid Apple enrolment, which is what would let iOS run on hardware early; whether the twelve-tester closed test is required per app or per developer account, which decides whether two listings cost two fourteen-day waits; and whether a Google Cloud billing account can be opened without a registered entity, which maps need and which MapLibre is the fallback for.
- **A food-regulatory adviser's review of the excluded-goods list**, and the platform's own FSSAI Central licence as an e-commerce food business: both belong to stage two, and neither exists yet. The seller-level FSSAI requirement for shops selling through an operator is unsettled across sources.
- **Every pricing rate, fee, floor, delivery split, credit limit and booking fee**: no value is chosen yet. The first rate card must exist before the first shop goes Live and is revised from real orders afterwards; see Pricing engine.
- **Date for the catalog and synonym seed package.** The founder owns it: roughly 300 to 500 grocery and kirana master products with quantity-price options, brands and transliterated synonyms, prepared with AI assistance, loaded through the admin importer, prioritising what the pilot shops sell, and validated before live ordering opens. The deadline is set against the pilot launch date, which is itself unset. It gates the pilot and is the only critical-path item that is not code.
